Frozen Shrimp Buying Specifications: Count Units, Product Form and Packing

Published 2026-10-27 · Updated 2026-10-27
Published by Yantai Dingdingmiao Seafood

To compare frozen shrimp offers, specify the delivered product form, count range and unit, measurement basis, net seafood weight and packing together. A label such as “31/40” cannot establish an equivalent product on its own.

In this guide
  1. Write the form before the count
  2. Read 31/40 with its unit and measurement basis
  3. Make weight and packing part of the same specification
  4. An example brief and a practical reply check

Write the form before the count

Begin with the species or product identity, then spell out the delivered form: head on or off, shell on or peeled, deveined or not, tail on or off, and raw or cooked. State whether you want individually frozen pieces or blocks. Ask the supplier to write back the same description; an abbreviation alone leaves room for misunderstanding.

Published specifications demonstrate why this matters. S&P Global's 2024 Platts shrimp assessment FAQ describes a head-on, shell-on example in pieces per kilogram and a peeled, deveined, tail-on example in pieces per pound. These are examples of particular assessment specifications, not a universal grading rule or an offer from Dingdingmiao.

Read 31/40 with its unit and measurement basis

“31/40” is incomplete until the supplier states the count unit. For an agreed specification of 31–40 pieces per pound, the range describes the count for that mass of the specified product. An offer of 31–40 pieces per kilogram uses a different mass basis. Ask how samples are prepared, whether ice glaze is removed, and what procedure and tolerance will be used to judge conformity.

Illustrative unit conversion: using approximately 0.4535924 kg per pound from NIST's conversion table, divide 31 and 40 by 0.4535924. The result is approximately 68.3–88.2 pieces per kilogram for the same product and measurement basis. This arithmetic does not create a new commercial grade or replace the agreed sampling method.

Do not use this conversion to predict the grade after removing the head, peeling or cooking. Those operations change the product. Ask for the finished-product count and a sample specification instead of assuming an equivalent yield. A seller's size name, such as “large,” is also insufficient without the numerical specification.

Make weight and packing part of the same specification

Record the seafood weight excluding glaze, any glaze requirement and its calculation basis, the inner-pack quantity and the number of packs per carton. Keep that separate from gross shipping weight and from the unit used to calculate the invoice. Use our glaze and net-weight comparison guide when two prices appear to use different weight bases.

Ask the supplier to identify treatments or added ingredients and provide the product information relevant to your destination and customer requirements. Mark unresolved label or document requirements as open. A generic packing photograph does not establish the contents or approval status of your proposed order.

If the offered pack differs from your request, compare it as a named alternative. For example, a change in inner-pack weight may affect how your customer handles the product even when the total carton weight is unchanged.

An example brief and a practical reply check

The following is a hypothetical buying requirement, not available stock, a minimum order quantity or a quotation. Replace every example field with your own requirement.

Product identity: vannamei shrimp; supplier to confirm scientific name
Delivered form: raw, peeled, deveined, tail on
Count: 31–40 pieces per pound of the specified finished product
Measurement: agree sample preparation, glaze removal and tolerance
Freezing: individually frozen pieces requested
Packing: 10 bags per carton; 1 kg net seafood per bag, excluding glaze
Glaze: supplier to state percentage and denominator for buyer review
Quantity: illustrative request of 1,000 kg net seafood
Destination country and port: buyer to complete
Timing, delivery term, documents and label needs: to be agreed
Reply with: exact match / proposed alternative / still unconfirmed

Before approving a sample, ask the supplier to return a dated specification showing the same product form, count basis and packing. Record which points the sample is intended to verify. Keep its reference with the measurements and supplier response so you can compare like with like.

Before an order, resolve changes separately: what changed, why it was proposed, its effect on the quotation, and whether the buyer accepted it. A sample review alone does not confirm the entire shipment, current availability or all destination requirements. The RFQ response-review checklist helps keep open points visible.

Buyer questions

Does 31/40 shrimp mean the same size in every offer?

No. Confirm whether the count is per pound or per kilogram, the product form and the measurement method. A number without those details is incomplete.

Can a head-on shrimp count predict the peeled product count?

A unit conversion cannot establish processing yield or the finished count grade. Request the finished specification and a representative sample measured on an agreed basis.
