Frozen Mackerel Specifications: Five Fields for a Clear Inquiry

Published 2026-06-17 · Updated 2026-10-23
Published by Yantai Dingdingmiao Seafood

A useful mackerel inquiry identifies the species, preparation, size basis, packing and destination. Put units beside every size and weight, and mark any alternative species or form as a separate proposal. Avoid choosing a grade solely because another market is said to prefer it.

In this guide
  1. 1. Identity
  2. 2. Product form
  3. 3. Size and tolerance
  4. 4. Freezing, weight and packing
  5. 5. Destination and use

1. Identity

Specify the scientific name if you have it and request confirmation against the proposed product records. “Mackerel” can be used broadly in commercial conversation, so the supplier should not decide the species silently. State whether alternatives are acceptable and ask for them to be quoted separately.

2. Product form

Describe whole round, gutted, headed-and-gutted, butterfly, fillet or the exact preparation you need. Spell out abbreviations in the agreed specification. A change of preparation can change packing, yield and downstream handling, so it must be visible in the comparison.

3. Size and tolerance

State individual weight, length or a count basis as appropriate, including its unit. For example, a buyer may request a specified gram range per whole fish; that range is the buyer’s requirement, not a universal market standard. Agree how out-of-range pieces and sample results will be handled.

4. Freezing, weight and packing

Describe whether you need separate frozen pieces or an agreed block, the seafood weight excluding glaze, inner packs and carton configuration. If the location or timing of freezing matters, ask for the supporting information. “Sea-frozen” or “land-frozen” alone does not prove the condition of the proposed lot.

5. Destination and use

Tell the supplier whether the product will be sold, repacked or processed and identify the country and delivery point. Provide customer requirements for review, and have the importer confirm the applicable product and document checks. Do not assume one document list applies to all destinations.

An initial brief can read: “Frozen mackerel, species to be confirmed against our requirement; whole round; buyer-specified g per fish and tolerance; net seafood kg per carton; packing and glaze basis stated; quantity, destination and timing attached.” Replace each undecided item before final order agreement.

Buyer questions

Is a country name enough to select the correct mackerel size?

No. Use your actual customer demand and intended application to define a size with units and tolerance, then check available options.

Does a freezing description prove product quality?

No. It is one part of the specification. Request the relevant lot information and agree the product checks needed for your order.
