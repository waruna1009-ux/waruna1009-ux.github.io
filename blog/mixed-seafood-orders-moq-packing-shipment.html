Mixed Seafood Orders: Check MOQ, Packing and Shipment Readiness

Published 2026-11-13 · Updated 2026-11-13
Published by Yantai Dingdingmiao Seafood

Request a mixed seafood order as separate product lines plus one shared shipment brief. Combining fish, shrimp or squid in a buying request does not confirm that they can travel together. First resolve each line's specification, supplier minimum, packing and ready date; then ask the responsible parties to confirm a compatible loading plan, documents, delivery scope and costs.

  2. Build a line-by-line requirement sheet

Separate the minimums before adding quantities

Ask what each quoted minimum applies to. A supplier's product minimum, a particular processing or size batch, printed packaging and a proposed shipment can have different conditions. Write the answer against the exact line; do not assume that quantities of different species or forms can be added together to satisfy it.

  • Product line: Which species, form, size and packing does the proposed minimum cover? Can size grades be combined, or must each meet its own requirement?
  • Packaging: Does the offer use standard packs or buyer artwork? Ask about separate printing/setup charges and responsibility for any unused packaging.
  • Shipment: Who will confirm the load's actual weight, volume, handling and route? A target order weight alone does not prove that a container is full, suitable or booked.

Dingdingmiao does not publish a universal mixed-order minimum. Feasibility and terms must be investigated for the requested combination. We coordinate seafood sourcing and quotations; we do not promise an owned consolidation warehouse or a scheduled mixed-container service.

Keep these fields separate: net seafood weight is not gross transport weight. Use the RFQ and supplier-response comparison sheet to record the requirement beside the offered answer.

Check the shared shipment as its own decision

Even when every line has an offer, the combined shipment needs a separate review. Ask the proposed logistics provider and relevant suppliers to confirm:

  1. Compatibility and handling: required carriage conditions for every line, packaging protection, segregation where needed, and the actual stowage plan. Do not choose a shared temperature merely because all items are described as frozen.
  2. Readiness: where each line will be available, its confirmed date, the collection/loading sequence and who is responsible during storage and transfer.
  3. Product and route records: map each line to its supplier, production lot, origin and applicable documents. Have the importer and appropriate advisers verify destination requirements for the specific combination; one line's documents do not establish another line's eligibility.
  4. Commercial scope: identify the contracting seller or sellers, the coordinator, carrier and importer. List collection, handling, storage, inspection and other quoted charges separately, including who bears additional costs if one line is delayed.

Hapag-Lloyd's reefer handling guidance calls for product temperature to match transport conditions before loading, suitable packaging and an unobstructed air path. It also cautions against loading above the load line or beyond the T-bar floor. These are reasons to obtain a load-specific plan, not evidence that any proposed product combination has been accepted by a carrier.

Record the chosen delivery rule, named place and edition in the quotation. ICC explains that Incoterms rules allocate specified obligations, risks and costs; they do not settle product specifications, payment terms or ownership transfer. Document those points separately with the relevant parties.

Choose what to do with an unresolved line

In the illustration, suppose lines A and B have matching offers but C still has no confirmed source. Treat C as unresolved, even if the overall target quantity seems convenient.

  • Wait for C: request refreshed ready dates, validity and any storage implications for A and B before deciding.
  • Separate C: ask for the revised quantities, route and total cost for A and B, plus a separate proposal for C. Do not assume the original freight allocation still applies.
  • Consider an alternative: keep the alternate species, form or pack in a separate version and obtain the buyer's explicit acceptance before it replaces C.
  • Leave C out: record it as not sourced and revise the scope. Removing a line changes the buying plan; it is not proof that the remaining shipment is ready.

Use a simple status for every line: requirements open, offer under review, accepted for the agreed scope or not sourced. Add the decision owner and next step. A missing answer should never become an assumed approval.

Copy a mixed-order brief

Send one copy of the product fields for each line, followed by the shared shipment questions. Write “not decided” where information is missing.
